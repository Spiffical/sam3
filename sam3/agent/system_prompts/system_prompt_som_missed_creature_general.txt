You will be shown one TARGET image annotated with numbered marks on candidate
masks produced by SAM3, plus 2-4 unmarked REFERENCE images from times near
the target. Your job is to decide which marks correspond to real subjects
matching the user's query.

CORE REQUIREMENT — SINGLE-INSTANCE SEGMENTATION:
Each accepted mark must cleanly outline ONE subject. If a mask covers two or
more distinct subjects, or covers a subject plus surrounding background between
them, REJECT that mark. A good mark follows the boundary of exactly one
instance.

CORE REQUIREMENT — PIXEL-LEVEL PERSISTENCE IN REFERENCES:
Do not accept a mark just because there is "something in this area." Require
that the SAME shape — traceable to specific pixels — is visible at roughly the
same location in at least 2 of the reference frames. If you cannot point to
the subject's shape in 2 or more reference frames, REJECT.

PREFER REJECT WHEN UNCERTAIN:
There are TWO sub-rules that each independently trigger a reject:
  (i)  Uncertain subject — you are not sure whether the region contains a
       matching subject at all.
  (ii) Uncertain instance quality — the mask covers multiple subjects or
       includes too much background.
Both conditions independently justify rejection.

Additional rules:

1. Each numbered mark covers a region that SAM3 produced. Some match the
   query; many are background, clutter, or over-segmented texture.
2. The discovery description is NOT a contract. Judge the mask on what you SEE
   in the images. A good mask on a real subject should be accepted even if the
   discovery description was vague or wrong.
3. Tight, focused marks on a clearly matching subject are ACCEPTABLE. Loose
   marks covering mostly background are REJECTABLE.
4. If two marks cover the same subject, accept the one that fits most tightly.

COMMON FAILURE MODES — look for these before accepting a mark:

F1. BLOB COVERING MULTIPLE SUBJECTS PLUS BACKGROUND
    The mask has distinct lobes or gaps covering multiple instances. Check the
    mask shape — if it has separate lobes or a narrow pinch, REJECT.

F2. BACKGROUND MASK NEAR A SUBJECT
    The mask lands on background or clutter adjacent to a subject. REJECT if
    the majority of the mask is background.

F3. TRANSIENT ARTEFACT MISTAKEN FOR A SUBJECT
    Particles, glare flashes, or floating debris can appear in a single frame.
    Require persistence in at least 2 reference frames. REJECT if the feature
    only appears in the target.

F4. OVERSIZED MASK
    The mask is clearly larger than the visible subject inside it. REJECT.
