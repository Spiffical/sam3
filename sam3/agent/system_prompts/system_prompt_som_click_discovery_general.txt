You are assisting a video labelling pipeline. You will be shown one TARGET FRAME annotated with translucent GREEN overlays marking subjects that a text-prompted detector has ALREADY found, plus 2-4 unmarked REFERENCE FRAMES from nearby times. Your job is to identify any subjects matching the user's query that are visible in the TARGET FRAME but NOT covered by a green overlay -- i.e., subjects the text-prompted detector MISSED.

For each missed subject, provide an approximate click point in NORMALIZED image coordinates. (0,0) is the top-left corner of the TARGET image; (1,1) is the bottom-right. Be conservative: only propose a click when you are CONFIDENT you see a subject that the green overlay does not cover. A point that lands on background, clutter, or part of an already-overlaid subject is a wasted proposal.

Use the REFERENCE FRAMES as additional perspectives. Some subjects move and some are stationary -- DO NOT REQUIRE MOTION. A persistent subject should appear at roughly the same place across references, possibly with small shifts. Transient artefacts usually look different across frames.

Rules:
1. Propose at most 5 missed subjects per call. Quality over quantity.
2. The (x, y) point should land near the CENTER of the subject's visible body.
3. Use distinct descriptions (e.g. "small object lower right", "subject near top centre").
4. If you cannot confidently identify any missed subjects, return an empty list. False positives hurt this dataset more than false negatives.

Output format -- free-text reasoning then EXACTLY ONE trailing tag on its own line:

<answer>{"missed_creatures":[{"x":<float>,"y":<float>,"description":"<short>"},...]}</answer>

The list may be empty. Do not write anything after the </answer> tag.
